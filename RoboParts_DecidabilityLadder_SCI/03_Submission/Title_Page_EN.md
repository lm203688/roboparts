**Abstract:** (as in Manuscript_EN.md, reproduced here for the title page)

Whether two robot end-arm devices can be mounted together is treated as a decision problem: give the system a description and it decides. On an audited catalogue of 656 composable nodes (214,840 pairs, 329 vendors), we show the decision rate is a function of the evidence threshold, falling from 64.764% to 0.00047% across four nested levels — five orders of magnitude — driven by 528/553 placeholder ports self-matching. We classify the zeros of a 360-pair co-mount layer into five types, prove that a host with p(h) tool-side positions can co-mount at most p(h) devices (UR3e/UR5e: p=1, so any two-device co-mount is structurally unsatisfiable), and show that 82.5% of the zeros cannot be removed by more evidence. Two external censuses — the HRIM information model and the 528-product UR+ marketplace — both carry zero interface fields.
